# GlobeView data sources and behavior

**Armenia road notices:** [Road Department](https://armroad.am/am/press/urgentnews) notices add dated work windows and recent bridge-closure reports. The server reads each page at most once per ten minutes across users; work windows expire at their UTC+4 end. Points are approximate numbered-road references near explicitly named settlements, from OpenStreetMap (ODbL) and GeoNames (CC BY 4.0). Published kilometre markers remain in the popup; precise sections are unverified. Closure reports without end dates state their publication date and unknown current status. [Asia rollout notes](ASIA_FEEDS.md#armenia--initial-integration-3-october-2026) describe coverage limits and rebuilding the offline catalog.

**Armenia emergency reports:** the [MIA Rescue Service](https://rescue.mia.gov.am/) supplies dated public incident reports through the shared five-minute emergency cache. Points use unique, province-scoped [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/) settlement references (CC BY 4.0), not precise incident locations. Completed responses are labelled; this is not live dispatch coverage. The adapter omits stale reports and ambiguous locations. Partial failures elsewhere remain in API diagnostics and the console without generating unrelated country warnings over the map; a failed overall request still shows an error.

**Yerevan road-camera inventory** adds 106 named junction references from the [Armenia Interior Ministry](https://mia.gov.am/certificate/) under **Plate readers & enforcement**. The source document was modified in May 2026; equipment status is unverified. These are approximate junction references, not public video streams or confirmed plate readers. Locations come from matching every published road name to a shared [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) node (ODbL 1.0). The small catalog loads once per server process and is filtered to the viewport; viewers cause no source downloads or geocoding. [Asia rollout notes](ASIA_FEEDS.md#yerevan-road-camera-inventory) record unresolved source entries and rebuild instructions.

GlobeView opens on a spherical world globe rendered by a single MapLibre GL JS map. You can rotate and zoom anywhere, jump to a region, collapse the layer panel, switch to a flat projection, or enable 3D terrain and buildings. The home globe rotates automatically; interacting with the map stops it. Shared zoomed-in links and reduced-motion preferences start still. Rotation pauses while the page is hidden. The map controls also rotate the bearing in 45° steps. The 3D switch loads Mapterhorn elevation tiles only when enabled; aircraft markers stay on the map surface. Reported altitude remains available in aircraft details. The Alps shortcut shows the relief at a useful zoom. The worldwide basemap comes from OpenFreeMap.

All layers render in the same MapLibre view: worldwide aircraft, live vessels, natural hazards, cybersecurity signals, public webcams, world radio, and the existing road, utility, emergency, and weather feeds. Operational point features appear as **individual icons without clustering**; radio stations appear as individual small dots. Dense feeds wait until a useful zoom level. Kazakhstan’s QazAvtoJol repair restrictions also draw their affected road segments; clicking a marker or line opens the operator’s notice. Road camera popups show snapshots and available video. Kazakhstan’s two verified KazToll toll-plaza cameras play recent looping MP4 clips inside the map, refreshed while playing. Their points are approximate toll-plaza locations; stale clips are suppressed. The public webcam catalog has eight direct HLS feeds that play in the popup, two IPCamLive cameras that show a two-minute preview and attempt the operator's embedded player, and one RTSP.me embed. The embedded player keeps the preview and operator link if it cannot load. Four [Shahdag resort panoramas](https://www.shahdag.az/en/live-camera) in Azerbaijan open the operator’s PANOMAX interactive player inside the popup on request. These show recent panoramic captures with operator timestamps, rather than a continuous traffic-camera feed; vendor branding, archive controls, and fullscreen remain available. The remaining 187 SkylineWebcams popups use Skyline's official still image, updated about every five minutes; Skyline only permits camera hosts to embed live video, so these popups link to the operator for live viewing.

[ElCat](https://kg.camera/ru/) supplies 19 public road and city camera views in Kyrgyzstan at zoom 10+. Fresh previews update once a minute and live video plays inside the popup on request. Cameras with stale or ended streams are hidden; locations are supplied by the operator. © ElCat, reused with attribution and source links.

[Kyrgyztelecom](https://online.kt.kg/) supplies three live street views of Bishkek's Ala-Too Square, shown at one approximate square reference in Road cameras at zoom 10+. The popup switches angles and plays the selected live stream. Previews are decoded from a current video segment and cached across users for one minute. The operator permits retransmission with its name and source link. The reference location is OpenStreetMap way `181568920` (ODbL 1.0), not a surveyed camera position.

[City of Bishkek road notices](https://www.bishkek.gov.kg/ru/post) add dated construction restrictions at zoom 10+. Published named street sections and landmarks are matched to audited OpenStreetMap references (ODbL 1.0); unresolved locations are omitted. These are announced restriction windows, not a complete live road-status feed. Exact hours or explicit day durations are required, and expired work disappears. One shared five-minute refresh checks the first three news pages and revalidates retained active notices; requests are restricted to the city host, refuse redirects, and cap page and notice counts. Cached markers expire within 15 minutes during a source failure. Verified sections are drawn along their reference road geometry.

**World radio** uses the open [Radio Browser directory](https://docs.radio-browser.info/). Turn on Radio stations and drag the globe until its fixed target sits over a station dot; releasing the globe tunes the nearest station. The Tune target switch hides the reticle and disables drag-to-tune while leaving radio dots clickable. Click a dot directly to tune it, or choose from the nearby-station list when several share the target area. The player stays open while moving around the globe or switching map layers. The server keeps a deduplicated catalog of geocoded, last-reported-working HTTPS non-HLS streams, refreshes it at most every 12 hours, and serves its last snapshot when the directory is temporarily unavailable. The catalog currently prioritizes the 8,000 most clicked geocoded entries, so it is not a complete station census. Browser playback can still fail when a broadcaster's stream goes offline, blocks the listener's region, or uses an unsupported codec. The browser connects directly to the broadcaster for audio; GlobeView sends a station-click report to Radio Browser only after playback begins, as requested by its API documentation. Station locations are directory entries and may be approximate or incorrect.

Each page load starts with radar, both aircraft types, vessels, public webcams, road cameras, tropical cyclones, earthquakes, all road layers, and emergency reports in North America and abroad enabled. Weather alerts start off. Cameras, road markers, and emergency reports wait until zoom 10; traffic flow starts at zoom 6. Each layer-group heading has an **All** control. Startup defaults are marked with `data-default-on` in `index.html` and used by `globe.js` to initialize the map and switch states.

Aircraft overview positions come from OpenSky worldwide and a military supplement from ADSB.lol, refreshed every 30 minutes to leave room in OpenSky's anonymous daily credit allowance. Markers are spatially thinned at globe scale for readability; zoom 6+ uses a viewport query from ADSB.lol, refreshed about every 45 seconds. Civil aircraft are white; medical, law enforcement, fire/rescue, military, and other government aircraft retain distinct colors. Rotorcraft use helicopter silhouettes when the ADS-B category or aircraft type identifies them. To track one aircraft, select its map marker and choose **Track aircraft & show route**, or enter its callsign, tail number, or six-digit ICAO hex code in the main search bar. Tracking checks [ADSB.lol's specific-aircraft endpoint](https://github.com/adsblol/api) every 20 seconds and continues beyond the viewport. When that lookup fails, the server uses a rate-limited OpenSky ICAO lookup at most once per ten minutes per aircraft, subject to a shared daily budget. A failed viewport refresh keeps the last positions briefly and labels them as delayed rather than clearing the map. The highlighted marker and card show reported position, altitude, speed, heading, squawk, vertical rate, source, and signal age when available; panning pauses camera follow, and Stop ends tracking. On selection, the map loads the current flight's **observed trail** from [OpenSky's live tracks endpoint](https://openskynetwork.github.io/opensky-api/rest.html), then extends the line with positions observed during that tracking session. **View flown trail** frames the observed path on the map. The server caches trail lookups for ten minutes and caps them to protect OpenSky's separate anonymous track allowance. The trail may be absent or incomplete where OpenSky has no coverage; it is not a planned route. For selected aircraft with a plausible [ADSB.im](https://adsb.im/) callsign route, the map separately draws a solid straight connector from the departure airport to the current position and a dashed connector toward the destination. **View full route** fits both airports on screen. The airport pair is not a filed flight plan and may be absent or mismatched when callsigns are reused. [adsb.fi's open-data API](https://github.com/adsbfi/opendata) was reviewed but is not used: its public endpoints are limited to personal, non-commercial use and its worldwide snapshot is feeder-only. Coverage still depends on receiver networks. The public webcam catalog contains 198 operator links across 48 countries, derived from [OSIRIS](https://github.com/simplifaisoul/osiris); live feed availability can change. Road and utility feeds remain regional.

**Roads abroad** adds Finland's [Fintraffic / Digitraffic](https://www.digitraffic.fi/en/road-traffic/) traffic announcements, active roadworks, and recently updated variable signs, active London road disruptions from [Transport for London](https://api-portal.tfl.gov.uk/products), current incidents and roadworks from [Traffic Wales RSS](https://traffic.wales/developers), current geolocated Scottish works from the [Scottish Road Works Register](https://roadworks.scot/opendata), current incidents and works on France's non-concession national roads from [Bison Futé / DIR](https://transport.data.gouv.fr/datasets/evenements-routiers-sur-le-reseau-routier-national-non-concede) under Licence Ouverte 2.0, current Flemish roadworks and restrictions from the [Vlaams Verkeerscentrum](https://www.verkeerscentrum.be/data), and local work zones with road impacts from [GIPOD](https://geo.api.vlaanderen.be/GIPOD/ogc/features/v1) under its free-reuse model licence. French and Flemish DATEX II publications must be recent, and only events within their validity windows appear. Flemish Lambert 72 coordinates are transformed on the server. GIPOD is queried only for current, validated road impacts in small viewport tiles with a shared five-minute cache. The Welsh feed covers motorways and trunk roads. The Scottish disruption extract is updated daily under OGL v3, so it is less current than a live control-centre feed; the server downloads it once per day, keeps active records in memory, and sends only points in the user's current map view. Finland feeds require gzip requests and identify GlobeView with a `Digitraffic-User` header; the server caches a shared snapshot for three minutes. Finnish records are adapted from Fintraffic data under [CC BY 4.0](https://www.digitraffic.fi/en/terms-of-service/). TfL's anonymous quota is 50 requests per minute; the shared cache makes at most one request per three-minute refresh. Road events remain limited to the publishing authorities' coverage and are shown as representative points. Road sensors also show fresh speed and vehicle-count measurements from the [French Bison Futé / DIR feed](https://transport.data.gouv.fr/datasets/etat-de-circulation-en-temps-reel-sur-le-reseau-routier-national-non-concede), where its station reference has coordinates. The reference is refreshed twice daily; measurements older than 30 minutes are hidden. Active [Zurich cantonal counters](https://datenkatalog.statistik.zh.ch/datasets/692@tiefbauamt-kanton-zuerich) appear with a labelled historical daily average and an on-demand recent vehicle detection, not a flow estimate. Traffic flow has not gained an international source.

**Armenia planned power work:** [Electric Networks of Armenia](https://www.ena.am/Info.aspx?id=5&lang=1) supplies upcoming work dates, UTC+4 hours, utility districts, and affected addresses. One hourly source fetch is shared across users. Every snapshot removes expired windows; records also carry a 15-minute validity limit. Only the current planned section is read, excluding the legacy 2025 breakdown table. The source omits the year, so dates must resolve uniquely within the current local day and following seven days. [GeoNames](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/AM.zip) ([CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)) supplies approximate settlement and Yerevan district references, not customer outage footprints. Unknown or conflicting locations are omitted; the popup retains the published addresses. Rebuild the offline catalog with `scripts/update_armenia_outage_locations.py` and a downloaded `AM.txt`.

**Azerbaijan planned power work:** [Azerishiq's monthly IDDA schedule](https://opendata.az/en/@azerisiq-asc/elektrik-techizatinda-planli-fasileler) (CC0) supplies dates, hours, work descriptions, and affected addresses. The server retrieves the current CSV link through the published dataset API, sharing one hourly metadata/CSV refresh across users. Active and upcoming seven-day windows use UTC+4 and expire at the published end; ambiguous dates, unknown network scopes, and unmatched addresses are omitted. Named cities and villages are approximate reference points from [GeoNames](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/AZ.zip) ([CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)), not customer outage footprints or confirmed live faults. The popup retains the published affected area. Baku utility district scopes come from the [operator directory](https://www.azerishiq.az/menu/unvanlar); their names must not match unrelated villages elsewhere. Rebuild the offline catalog with `scripts/update_azerbaijan_outage_locations.py` and a downloaded `AZ.txt`.

**Netherlands roads:** [NDW Open Data](https://opendata.ndw.nu/) supplies current DATEX II road situations and working digital signs. The server checks publication age and event validity, converts WGS84 line coordinates to representative points, and shares a three-minute cache across users. Where NDW supplies a current sign image, the popup displays it; blank and nonworking signs are omitted.

**Germany motorways:** The [Autobahn GmbH API](https://verkehr.autobahn.de/o/autobahn/) supplies current warnings, closures, and roadworks on the Autobahn network. Future records are excluded. Warnings share a five-minute server cache; works and closures share fifteen-minute caches. These are representative event points, not complete road closures or all German streets.

**Norway roads, cameras, and sensors:** [Statens vegvesen's open DATEX II WFS](https://git.vegvesen.no/projects/DATEX2/repos/datex2-spesifications/raw/3.1/NPRA_DATEXII_3_1_Specification_WFS-WMS_v1.1.pdf) supplies active road situations, roadside camera locations, recent road-weather observations, and reported segment travel times under NLOD. Available still images appear in camera popups at zoom 10+. Road sensors include readings no more than an hour old and travel times no more than 20 minutes old. The server checks publication freshness and shares a three-minute snapshot; individual camera images may lag or be unavailable.

**Northern Ireland roads and cameras:** [TrafficWatch NI](https://www.trafficwatchni.com/twni/cameras) provides geolocated roadworks, readable variable-message signs, and camera stills under its [Crown copyright / OGL notice](https://www.trafficwatchni.com/twni/crown-copyright). GlobeView polls the public map once every three minutes for all viewers, displays works in their published date window and sign messages updated in the last 30 minutes, and caches selected camera images for one minute. Its Republic of Ireland sign records are excluded from this source. The stills are not video; a camera can be offline.

**Republic of Ireland roads and cameras:** [TII Traffic](https://traffic.tii.ie/) supplies geolocated active camera stills and road events. GlobeView shows current incidents and works, audits cameras hourly to omit stale images, and serves selected images through a shared cache. Readable sign images appear only if the display file was modified within 30 minutes. TII information is reused under its [CC BY 4.0 public sector licence](https://www.tii.ie/en/compliance/reuse-of-public-sector-information/); the required attribution is in the app's source details.

**Plate readers & enforcement** includes the worldwide [DeFlock](https://deflock.me/) / [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) region index. The server retrieves only the 20° source tiles intersecting the current zoomed-in viewport, including views across the date line, caches each tile for 15 minutes, and returns at most 10,000 mapped locations for that view. These are community-mapped device locations, not a live read feed, an exhaustive inventory, or a statement that a camera is active. Uzbekistan also includes 1,395 distinct locations from the Interior Ministry's [1 January 2025 enforcement inventory](https://gov.uz/oz/iiv/news/view/34435): 1,163 cameras and 232 radars. These are explicitly labelled enforcement devices, without an ALPR or public-video claim. The source's decimal commas and split latitude rows are normalized, duplicate coordinate/type rows are merged, and one location outside Uzbekistan's OSM national boundary is omitted. This fixed inventory loads once per server process, creates no upstream polling, and is filtered to the current viewport at zoom 10+. Every popup states the inventory date and that current status is unverified. `scripts/update_uzbek_enforcement.py` reproduces the catalog from the official HTML and country boundary.

**Power outages abroad** adds current fault data from [UK Power Networks Live Faults](https://ukpowernetworks.opendatasoft.com/explore/dataset/ukpn-live-faults/), [Northern Powergrid Live Power Cut Data](https://northernpowergrid.opendatasoft.com/explore/dataset/live-power-cuts-data/), [SSEN PowerTrack](https://data.ssen.co.uk/@ssen-distribution/realtime_outage_dataset), [National Grid Electricity Distribution](https://connecteddata.nationalgrid.co.uk/dataset/live-power-cuts), [NIE Networks Powercheck](https://powercheck.nienetworks.co.uk/), and [Liander](https://data.overheid.nl/dataset/storingsdata-liander-actuele-storingen). The server refreshes these feeds at most every five minutes. It excludes restored and future planned UK Power Networks records and uses Northern Powergrid's `isaffected` flag. SSEN reports current faults across its southern England and northern Scotland service areas. NGED covers the Midlands, South West, and South Wales; records whose upload is more than two hours old are hidden. NGED data is used under its [open-data licence](https://www.nationalgrid.co.uk/open-data-licence) with the required attribution “Supported by NGED Open Data.” NIE contributes recently updated Northern Ireland cuts with approximate Irish Grid points. Liander contributes unresolved, recently updated unplanned electricity outages in its Dutch service area under CC BY 4.0. One approximate point represents each incident, not an affected-area boundary. [Alatau Zharyk Company](https://www.azhk.kz/ru/spetsialnye-razdely/avarijnye-otklyucheniya) contributes public transformer interruption points in the Almaty service area of Kazakhstan. The source snapshot must be at most 15 minutes old; interruption starts must be within 48 hours, and planned work must still be in its scheduled window. Expired cached points are removed even during provider failures. These are utility-reported points without customer counts or verified outage boundaries. [Bishkek PES](http://bipes.nesk.kg/ru/abonentam/perechen-uchastkov-rabot/) and [Issyk-Kul PES](http://ipes.nesk.kg/ru/kardarlarga/plandalgan-ish-ajmaktardyn-tizmesi/) add dated planned-work notices for Bishkek/nearby settlements and verified locations around Issyk-Kul, Kyrgyzstan. Issyk-Kul matches are scoped to the named electrical district; same-name villages from another district are excluded. Notices from today through the next seven days use curated OpenStreetMap building, street, or settlement reference points (ODbL 1.0), explicitly labeled as references rather than outage footprints. Future work is labeled Scheduled; an active schedule is a Planned work window, not confirmation of a live fault. Unmatched locations are omitted. Dates must agree with the full year in the notice heading; ended windows disappear. The shared five-minute loader refreshes schedules, and cached markers expire within 15 minutes if the source fails. These sources provide regional coverage in the UK, Netherlands, Kazakhstan, and Kyrgyzstan.

Live vessel positions come from [AISStream](https://aisstream.io/documentation). Add `AISSTREAM_API_KEY` to `.env` or the server environment. The server keeps one compressed WebSocket connection and combines active viewers' map areas at zoom 5+. Idle areas expire after 60 seconds; duplicate boxes are shared. To protect stream throughput, the defaults are 32 distinct boxes (`AISSTREAM_MAX_BOXES`) and 512 active viewers (`AISSTREAM_MAX_VIEWERS`). When full, new areas show BUSY instead of displacing existing viewers. The key stays on the server. AIS is event-driven, so positions can be sparse offshore.

Natural hazards are global report layers. [NASA EONET](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/v3) supplies open tropical cyclone events and their observed tracks (cached 15 minutes); [USGS](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/geojson.php) supplies magnitude 2.5+ earthquakes from the past seven days (cached two minutes); and [GDACS](https://www.gdacs.org/Documents/2025/GDACS_API_quickstart_v2.pdf) supplies current major wildfire and flood reports plus volcanic reports from the past 30 days (cached 30 minutes). Selecting a cyclone loads on-demand forecast guidance: [NHC ATCF](https://ftp.nhc.noaa.gov/atcf/aid_public/) for North Atlantic and eastern/central North Pacific storms, or [UCAR Tropical Cyclone Guidance Project](https://hurricanes.ral.ucar.edu/realtime/current/) for western North Pacific, North Indian Ocean, and Southern Hemisphere storms with a JTWC storm ID. The map distinguishes independent model tracks, the lighter GEFS ensemble-member paths, the GEFS mean, and an official forecast where one is published in the selected deck. It prefers a recent cycle with at least two model tracks; if those have not arrived, it can show a recent ensemble-only cycle. Named NHC storms resolve through NOAA’s current master storm list, shared across users and cached for 15 minutes. Guidance cycles older than 24 hours are not shown. Ended storms with no recent model runs show an unavailable message rather than archived forecasts. These lines are possible paths, not probabilities or safety instructions. Markers link to source reports and show observation time or report details when available. Fire and flood markers are event locations, not perimeters; volcanic reports may describe events that have ended. Reporting can lag conditions on the ground, and no layer is an emergency warning service.

**NWS alerts · U.S.** adds current National Weather Service watches, warnings, and advisories from its [active alerts API](https://api.weather.gov/alerts/active), refreshed about every two minutes. The map draws an alert polygon when NWS publishes one; zone-based alerts use a representative centroid from the [NWS GIS zone downloads](https://www.weather.gov/gis/publiczones) and [marine zone downloads](https://www.weather.gov/gis/MarineZones). A marker is not the full affected area. Alert popups link to the original NWS notice for complete coverage and instructions. The bundled `nws-zone-centroids.json` can be rebuilt with `python3 scripts/build_nws_zones.py` when NWS publishes new zone files (requires `pyshp`).

**International weather alerts** adds current notices from [Environment and Climate Change Canada](https://api.weather.gc.ca/collections/weather-alerts), [MetService New Zealand](https://alerts.metservice.com/cap/rss), [MET Norway](https://api.met.no/weatherapi/metalerts/2.0/documentation), [Met Éireann](https://data.gov.ie/dataset/weather-warnings), [DWD Germany](https://opendata.dwd.de/weather/alerts/cap/), [Azores Civil Protection](https://www.prociv.azores.gov.pt/), [PAGASA Philippines](https://publicalert.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/feeds/) (CC BY 4.0), [India NDMA SACHET](https://sachet.ndma.gov.in/CapFeed), the [Sri Lanka Department of Meteorology](https://was.meteo.gov.lk/api-docs), the [Maldives Meteorological Service](https://cap.meteorology.gov.mv/rss/alerts/), [MET Malaysia](https://developer.data.gov.my/realtime-api/weather), and [Kazhydromet Kazakhstan](https://meteoalert.meteoinfo.ru/kazakhstan/cap-feed/en/atom.xml). India includes official weather and river-flood CAP notices. SACHET publishes detailed affected-area polygons; the map shows an approximate point derived from each alert's largest published area to keep the shared feed small. Its CAP documents are revalidated with ETags as required by [NDMA's integration guide](https://sachet.ndma.gov.in/docs/Integration_Guide_For_Agencies.pdf), and the India snapshot refreshes at most every ten minutes. Canada, New Zealand, Norway, Germany, the Philippines, the Maldives, and Kazakhstan publish affected-area polygons. Sri Lanka uses CAP polygons when supplied and otherwise places approximate markers for named districts, using representative points from [geoBoundaries](https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbOpen/LKA/ADM2/) (OpenStreetMap/Wambacher, ODbL 1.0). Ireland lists affected counties; GlobeView joins these to generalized [Tailte Éireann county boundaries](https://data.gov.ie/dataset/counties-national-statutory-boundaries-2019-generalised-20m1) (CC BY 4.0). The server shares a roughly three-minute snapshot, preserves Met Éireann warning text, removes expired notices, and links to the original notice. PAGASA updates and all-clear messages supersede earlier notices. Marine warnings without county coverage are omitted. Malaysia uses approximate state reference points from [geoBoundaries](https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbOpen/MYS/ADM1/) (OpenStreetMap/Wambacher, ODbL 1.0) only for active warnings naming land states; marine-only notices are omitted. Kazakhstan’s and Kyrgyzstan’s public-domain CAP catalogs refresh in the background at most every five minutes; each immutable CAP file is fetched once per server cache. Kyrgyzstan uses [Kyrgyzhydromet’s WMO-registered English feed](https://alertingauthority.wmo.int/authorities.php?recId=83) with agency-published district polygons; English event and area labels can retain Russian advisory text. Valid 72-hour outlooks stay visible until their published expiry. The first catalog warmup can take about a minute, with its warnings appearing on a later shared alert refresh. Updates and cancellations supersede earlier notices, expiry is checked on every snapshot, and an unavailable source is omitted after 15 minutes without a successful refresh. A provider outage can leave one country temporarily unavailable.

[Tajikhydromet](https://www.meteo.tj/ru) regional weather bulletins appear as one national advisory at a clearly labelled Dushanbe capital reference. The source supplies text and calendar dates, without hazard polygons or an issuance timestamp. Notices expire in Tajikistan local time; international weather alerts remain off by default.

**Emergency reports · abroad** extends beyond the existing North American dispatch and alert layer with official [NSW Rural Fire Service current incidents](https://data.nsw.gov.au/data/dataset/nsw-rural-fire-service-current-incidents-feed), [Queensland Fire Department warnings](https://www.qld.gov.au/emergency/dealing-disasters/disaster-types/bushfires/bushfire-and-the-media/media-information-sources-for-bushfires), [Victoria emergency incidents](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/rss-feeds), [New Zealand Emergency Mobile Alerts](https://www.civildefence.govt.nz/guidance-training/guidelines/technical-standards/common-alerting-protocol), [England Environment Agency flood warnings](https://www.api.gov.uk/ea/flood-monitoring/), [LSZ Burgenland ongoing fire brigade dispatches](https://einsatz.lsz-b.at/), [Upper Austria fire brigade dispatches](https://einsaetze.ooelfv.at/publikationsformen), [Icelandic Meteorological Office active hazard alerts](https://api.vedur.is/), [Ústí nad Labem Region fire brigade reports](https://pkr.kr-ustecky.cz/pkr/zasahy-jednotek-pozarni-ochrany/), [Thailand DDPM disaster reports](https://ddc.disaster.go.th/), [Indonesia BNPB disaster reports](https://gis.bnpb.go.id/databencana/tabel/pencarian.php), and [Nepal BIPAD disaster reports](https://bipadportal.gov.np/api/). The server polls at most every five minutes, excludes NSW planned burns and old Victoria reports, and only maps alerts with valid coordinates. It may retain a source's last successful data for up to 15 minutes during an outage. Thailand records must be open, updated within three days, and started within 30 days; their points show reported disaster areas, not exact incident sites. Indonesia reports are dated within seven days and use approximate BNPB regency points, not incident coordinates. Nepal reports are approved and verified, reported within three days, and concern incidents within seven days; their supplied locations may be approximate. New Zealand alerts are shown when its official CAP feed has an active public message with a polygon or circle; it may normally be empty. Burgenland calls use approximate [Statistics Austria municipality population centers](https://data.statistik.gv.at/web/meta.jsp?dataset=OGDEXT_GEM_MP_1) (CC BY 4.0, attribution: Statistik Austria - data.statistik.gv.at) where a town name matches exactly. Upper Austria calls use the fire brigade's approximate public coordinates and exclude self-initiated operations that may be exercises. Iceland alerts use representative points from their published polygons; their source credit includes the retrieval date under the office's [reuse terms](https://en.vedur.is/about-imo/the-web/conditions). The Czech source publishes reported locations with delayed status updates and includes completed incidents from the last six hours. The map uses representative points, not full warning boundaries or exact addresses. These sources provide **regional coverage**, not a worldwide emergency dispatch network. Always follow the linked authority for action.

Zaragoza fire-service reports are also shown from the city's [same-day activity feed](https://www.zaragoza.es/web/espacio-de-datos/servicio/catalogo/1821). Its records have no incident coordinates; markers are approximate centers of uniquely matched short streets from the [municipal street dataset](https://www.zaragoza.es/web/espacio-de-datos/servicio/catalogo/279). Drills and ambiguous locations are omitted. Recently closed reports are labeled, not presented as ongoing calls.

The **satellite imagery** toggle opens on free reference imagery from [VersaTiles](https://docs.versatiles.org/basics/tilesets.html#satellite). Global imagery reaches about zoom 12. Above that, [USGS NAIP Plus](https://imagery.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/USGSNAIPPlus/ImageServer) provides aerial detail in the contiguous U.S., and VersaTiles supplies higher-resolution orthophotos in parts of Europe. Other places show an enlarged global tile at close zoom. Coverage and image dates vary; this does not provide current, driveway-level imagery worldwide. VersaTiles permits use of its hosted tiles for [prototypes and small projects](https://docs.versatiles.org/basics/tilesets.html); a larger public deployment should host regional extracts or arrange tile hosting. The sidebar also offers current- and previous-day [NASA GIBS Suomi NPP VIIRS true-color tiles](https://nasa-gibs.github.io/gibs-api-docs/access-basics/). Current-day tiles refresh every 30 minutes while visible, can have gaps, and may be cloudy. None of these views is live video. The **ports** layer is a local 2024 snapshot of the [NGA World Port Index](https://msi.nga.mil/Publications/WPI), taken from a [public ArcGIS copy of the January 2024 dataset](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=8ab783e55c324e1f94c77a255451c071). The layer shows medium and large ports at lower zoom, then all 3,831 entries from zoom 6, without clustering. It is reference data, not a live port feed.

**NASA FIRMS fire hotspots** displays the previous UTC day's NASA GIBS VIIRS 375 m thermal anomaly layer. These detections are not fire perimeters or individually confirmed fires. **GDELT conflict and protest reports** reads the latest 15-minute GDELT Event Database exports, with a three-hour lookback; GDELT automatically codes news coverage and reported geolocations can be approximate or administrative. **Day/night shading** is calculated locally from UTC solar position.

**ArcGIS explorer** adds nine optional, viewport-loaded reference layers: worldwide power plants from [WRI's Global Power Plant Database](https://www.wri.org/research/global-database-power-plants), active faults from the [GEM Foundation](https://www.globalquakemodel.org/product/active-faults-database), and [impact structures from an older ArcGIS archive](https://services.arcgis.com/e8gGAYmR5kxEFApE/ArcGIS/rest/services/Meteorites/FeatureServer/0); U.S. transmission lines and gas pipelines from [DOE NETL's hosted HIFLD copies](https://arcgis.netl.doe.gov/server/rest/services/Hosted); and wind turbines and solar footprints from the [USGS energy databases](https://energy.usgs.gov/). The GEM fault dataset is [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The DOE transmission copy cites 2022 data, the gas copy 2019 data, and the hosted fault copy was last edited in 2024. WRI plant records are historical, not a live generation feed. The impact archive includes probable sites and is not a current scientific census. Rail coverage combines a generalized [Natural Earth overview](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/railroads/) hosted on ArcGIS with detailed [FRA / BTS North American Rail Network lines](https://railroads.dot.gov/rail-network-development/maps-and-data/maps-geographic-information-system/maps-geographic) for the U.S., Canada, and Mexico (updated July 2026). The worldwide overview is uneven and may omit local tracks; neither rail layer shows live trains. None of these layers reports current grid operations, gas flow, or active fault movement. The server queries only a selected, validated bounding box and field list, caches results for one hour, and asks the viewer to zoom in when an area exceeds a provider's safe record limit.

The imagery selector also includes **Esri World Imagery** as a higher-detail global alternative. Esri describes coverage as approximately 0.3 m in select metropolitan areas, 0.5 m across the U.S. and parts of Western Europe, and 1 m across much of the rest of the world; image dates and resolution vary by area. Proper attribution is displayed on the map. Esri's no-charge online-service terms cover noncommercial use; commercial use requires a separate license. Do not use this source for offline tile export.

**Shipping routes · worldwide** overlays major, middle, and minor route corridors from Paul Benden's [Global Shipping Lanes dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/6361763) (CC BY 4.0). It was digitized from a CIA ocean map dated October 2012 and published in 2022. Route classes indicate approximate relative importance; they are not live AIS traffic, frequency measurements, or precise navigable tracks.

**GOES cloud imagery** is a separate weather toggle using NOAA GOES-East and GOES-West GeoColor frames through [NASA GIBS WMTS](https://nasa-gibs.github.io/gibs-api-docs/access-basics/). The map displays the latest available observation from each satellite and offers a six-frame, roughly 50-minute playback and a frame scrubber. Storm markers and forecast tracks remain above the imagery. The toggle only requests tiles while enabled; frame metadata refreshes every five minutes. GOES covers the Americas and much of the Pacific, not the whole globe. The panel shows the actual UTC observation times; processing delays and outages can occur. GeoColor is a cloud observation, not a forecast or live video.

**Sea conditions** is an on-demand map tool. Toggle it on and click an ocean location, or open a port and select **Check sea conditions**. It makes one [Open-Meteo Marine API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/marine-weather-api) request for the nearest sea grid cell and shows wave height, direction, period, sea surface temperature, and current when available. These are model forecasts, not measurements or navigation guidance. Open-Meteo's free API is for [non-commercial use with attribution and usage limits](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms); a commercial deployment needs the appropriate plan or another provider.

The main search bar supports cities, ports, `latitude, longitude`, aircraft callsigns, registrations, ICAO hex codes, and vessel names. Aircraft results start live tracking. Vessel-name results use recent AISStream reports already received by the server; the feed only collects the subscribed map area at zoom 5+, so this is not a worldwide vessel registry. Searching at zoom 5+ starts collection for the current area when the vessel layer is off. The place catalog is loaded only when search is used and is built from the [GeoNames cities15000 download](https://www.geonames.org/export/) (CC BY attribution) plus the port snapshot. Run `python3 scripts/build_global_catalogs.py` to refresh both static catalogs when the source data changes.

**Plan trip** opens a route planner for driving, walking, and cycling. Enter a city, port, or `latitude, longitude` in each field, or use the target buttons to pick exact start and destination points on the globe. The map draws the route with A/B markers and shows distance, estimated time, and selectable turn-by-turn steps. Place suggestions come from the bundled catalog; they do not use public Nominatim autocomplete. Short trips use the [FOSSGIS Valhalla public demo](https://valhalla.openstreetmap.de/). When that demo rejects a long driving trip at its 1,500 km limit, the planner makes one request to the [OSRM public demo](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Api-usage-policy) for an actual road route. Long walking and cycling trips show an explicit range message rather than claiming there is no connected route. The server caches identical routes for 15 minutes, limits requests, and identifies itself with `X-Client-Id` for Valhalla and a named User-Agent for OSRM. Set `GLOBALMAP_VALHALLA_URL` to an approved or self-hosted Valhalla `/route` endpoint and `GLOBALMAP_ROUTING_CLIENT_ID` to your application's identifier for a larger deployment. Both public demos are best-effort services; a production deployment should use its own routing backend. Route times do not account for live traffic, closures, or conditions; the planner provides trip estimates rather than live GPS navigation.

**Draw** adds private pins, text labels, multi-point lines, polygon areas, and radius-based circles. Choose a tool and click the map: lines and areas use Finish drawing, while circles use one click for the center and another for the edge. Select a drawing from the map or list to rename, recolor, locate, or delete it; a circle's radius can be edited precisely in miles or kilometers. Existing pins and areas remain readable. Drawings are stored only in the browser's `localStorage` under `globalmap.drawings.v1`; there is no server upload or cross-user sync. They reappear after reload on the same origin and browser profile. Another person using that same browser profile can see them, and clearing site data removes them. If browser storage is unavailable or full, the UI reports that the drawings will not persist.

Weather radar uses [RainViewer](https://www.rainviewer.com/api/weather-maps-api.html) worldwide when available, with a NOAA fallback for the United States. RainViewer's blue source tiles are recolored in the browser so lighter rain appears green while stronger returns remain yellow and red.

The cybersecurity section separates three public signals. **Observed scan sources** maps SANS Internet Storm Center's [top 100 source-IP list](https://www.dshield.org/feeds_doc.html), with approximate network locations from [RIPEstat](https://stat.ripe.net/docs/data-api/ripestat-data-api). The static list is preferred; when it contains fewer than 50 rows, the server uses [SANS's own top-IP API](https://isc.sans.edu/diary/Top100%2BMalicious%2BIP%2BSTIX%2BFeed/23050/) if it returns more records. At globe scale, the map shows a density glow counting nearby listed IPs and marks the top ten; zooming in shows individual source markers. The map key reports how many listed IPs were geolocated and offers a Focus control that temporarily hides other overlays. Cyber responses are also cached on disk for an hour, so server restarts and repeat visitors do not repeat the SANS fetch. Geolocation results are cached in memory for 24 hours and limited to six concurrent requests, below RIPEstat's eight-request limit. These are unfiltered firewall or honeypot reports, can contain false positives, and neither prove a successful hack nor identify an attacker’s physical location. The **Cyber briefing**, inspired by the structure of [FortiGuard's threat map](https://fortiguard.fortinet.com/threat-map), summarizes countries represented among those mapped SANS source IPs, recent items from [FortiGuard's published outbreak-alert RSS](https://filestore.fortinet.com/fortiguard/rss/outbreakalert.xml), and recent additions to [CISA’s KEV catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog). Country counts are IPs in this source list, not attack volumes or targeted-country rankings. Outbreak reports and KEV entries are not geolocated incidents. The app does not reproduce Fortinet's proprietary attack telemetry or draw invented attack routes. These features require no API key.

Armenia public webcams include the verified Northern Bus Station / Sevan highway operator stream. Playback is a click-to-load YouTube privacy-enhanced iframe; the marker is an approximate station reference. Stream IDs may change; see the operator and check date in `global-cameras.json`. Official road-camera network playback remains unverified.
